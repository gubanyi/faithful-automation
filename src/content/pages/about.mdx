Faithful Automation is a project kicked off by Marcus Gubanyi, an assistant professor of computer science at [Concordia University, Nebraska](https://cune.edu/). It emerged from recurring themes from conversations with Christians--students, educators, and other professionals--about the role of AI in their work and lives.

Some of these conversations about AI focus on its capability. Other conversations discuss the societal impacts of AI, the opportunities, the risks, and the future. But, all of the conversations are grounded in the hope we have in Christ and the purpose He provides for our lives.

Faithful Automation focuses on that Christ-centered view of AI in our vocations:

- *What is the purpose of this task, and why would I use AI for it?*
- *What is true, and how do I know?*
- *How is this shaping my thinking, and how am I using my time and gifts?*
- *What is this doing to my connections with other people?*

## Christ as the foundation

Faithful Automation is written from a Christian perspective. It starts from the conviction that we lack the wisdom to use AI faithfully on our own, and that Christ, who is faithful even when we aren't (2 Timothy 2:13), works through us in our vocations to serve our neighbors. The framework is an attempt to support Christians as we strive for faithfulness to God and to our neighbors. The questions are written to be useful to anyone.

## Case studies and perspectives

The [case studies](/case-studies/) and [perspectives](/perspectives/) on this site are based on the personal experience and views of their authors. So far, Marcus has written all of them. If you wish to share your case studies or perspectives, a submission form is in the works!

## Core convictions

- Christ is faithful, and the wisdom we need comes from God, not from our technologies.
- Our work is a vocation, a calling through which God serves our neighbors.
- Technology is not an authority or arbiter of truth.
- The frightening capabilities of frontier AI systems demand our attention and discernment.
- Gaining efficiency in our vocations is valuable but it is not the highest good.
- People are more than producers of output.
- Learning and growth often matter more than the finished product.
- Decisions about AI and automation should consider their effects on people and relationships.
- Discernment with regards to AI requires understanding, competency, wisdom, and context.
