Faithful Automation was developed by Dr. Marcus Gubanyi, Assistant Professor of Computer Science at [Concordia University, Nebraska](https://cune.edu/). It grew out of his desire to help students, educators, software developers, and other professionals think more carefully about the role of AI in their work and lives.

## A different starting point

Many conversations about technology begin with a question of capability:

*What can AI do?*

Faithful Automation begins with different questions:

- *What is the purpose of this task, and why would I use AI for it?*
- *What is true, and how do I know?*
- *How is this shaping the way I think?*
- *How is this affecting my relationships, and the joy I find in my work?*

Asking these questions first helps us treat powerful technologies as tools to be used wisely, not authorities to be obeyed.

## Core convictions

- Technology is a tool, not an authority.
- Efficiency is valuable, but it is not the highest good.
- People are more than producers of output.
- Learning and growth often matter as much as the finished product.
- Decisions about automation should consider their effects on people, relationships, and communities.
- Good judgment requires both wisdom and context.
